The short version

Most people building AI agents have never made software touch the physical world. For two years that was the only kind of software I wrote, and it is still what I design around: a constraint you cannot argue with, a deadline that is a public event, and a machine that either works in front of people or does not.

I compiled DLIB from source on a 1 GB Raspberry Pi because the wheels would not install, and the build took four hours. I designed a UART protocol between an Arduino Nano and a Pi because no library did what the joints needed. When an Arduino Mega hit its wireless ceiling I moved the whole codebase to ESP32 rather than wait. None of that is remarkable on its own. Together it is a habit: find the thing that is actually blocking, remove it, keep shipping.

Now I run that habit as a practice. A client brings a field I may not know, and seven days later there is something on a bench that runs.

Seven days Fixed

The sprint

Day one is reading and scoping: the vocabulary of the field, the constraint that actually binds, and the one question worth answering. Days two to four are a working prototype on real hardware, not a simulation. Days five to seven are hardening, documentation and handover. The deliverable is the thing that runs.

The ramp Stated plainly

Not every field moves at the same speed

Saying otherwise would be a claim you could check and find false. A domain that sits next to tech is productive in days, because the vocabulary is already shared and the failure modes are familiar. A domain far from tech takes longer, because the reading comes first and there is no shortcut through it. I scope and price against that curve rather than pretend it is flat.

Work

Jun 2023 – Apr 2024 RoboAI Hub

The intelligence layer of a humanoid that went on stage

The robot was built by a team led by the scientist Narayan Jangid over three years. I built its software: face recognition, the conversation engine and the speech pipeline. I was on stage when it was shown.

It ran on a Raspberry Pi 3B+ configured headless over SSH and VNC, with the Wi-Fi written into wpa_supplicant by hand. DLIB would not install, so I compiled it from source with CMake on the Pi itself, a four-hour build on 1 GB of RAM. The recognition module remembers who it has already greeted and answers team members differently from strangers. The conversation engine streams GPT-3.5-turbo with a persona that shifts by speaker, voiced through gTTS and ffmpeg. Raspbian Lite with a hand-installed lightweight desktop kept about seventy per cent of the memory for the application, which on 1 GB is the difference between a robot that runs and one that stops in front of an audience.

  • Compute budget 1 GB Raspberry Pi 3B+
  • DLIB build 4 hours, on the Pi
  • Press 4 regional papers
  • Shown Bheenmal, March 2024
Sep 2022 – May 2023 Government Polytechnic College, Jodhpur

A face-recognition attendance system, and the four people who built it

I led the team. The system runs fully locally with no AI APIs: DLIB and OpenCV for detection and recognition, a camera streamed over Wi-Fi so deployment did not depend on whatever hardware was in the room, a Tkinter desktop interface that works offline, and automated Excel session reports that replaced marking a register by hand.

The first problem was not the algorithm. Standard DLIB wheels failed on the lab machines, so I compiled it from source to unblock the pipeline before anyone wrote a line of recognition logic.

  • Team 4 people, led
  • Result 1st prize, best final-year project
June 2025 MBM University

KineLink, a gesture-controlled arm in seven days

I studied the DAWN remote-pilot system behind Japan's Avatar Robot Café, worked out how the piloting loop held together, and built my own version in seven days. A laptop, a camera, connections soldered on zero-PCB, and a base made from an empty tape roller. No lab and no equipment budget.

MediaPipe Pose tracks shoulder, elbow and wrist. I compute the joint angles with arctan2 geometry and map them to servo positions; MediaPipe Hands detects a closed fist and drives the gripper. Commands stream over a serial protocol I designed, index then angle then terminator, parsed by either an Arduino or an ESP32, so the arm is hardware-agnostic by design.

November 2024 RoboAI Hub

Servo control across two planes

An arm that moves through the XY and XZ planes with interpolation I wrote myself, driven wirelessly from a laptop over Bluetooth. It started on an Arduino Mega, hit that board's wireless ceiling, and I migrated the whole codebase to ESP32 rather than stall the build. No motion library did what was needed, so the angle-to-duty-cycle conversion is calculated by hand and the step-based interpolation is mine. PWM at 50 Hz, steady enough to run in front of people.

Shown three times: PROMETEO 2025 at IIT Jodhpur, where a professor recommended advancing the work; Taabar Mela in Jodhpur, in operation before an open public audience; and an MBM University faculty workshop on intelligent embedded systems.
